# Living-world acceptance requirements

The user's 2026-11-06 observation-world request supersedes the earlier timeline controls and fixed 12-hour product. The subsequent implementation authorization adds reliable river-city development, stable movement, evolving buildings, tracked cargo, an experience model and a connected environment. These IDs describe the accepted contract. See status.md for evidence, not this file, for completion claims.

| ID | Required behavior and acceptance |
| --- | --- |
| LIV-01 | One continuously advancing world. Opens directly into the 3D settlement, without play/pause, comparison, rewind or time-scrubber controls. Tick advances through observation panels/dialogs and beyond 720. |
| LIV-02 | Actual demand-led growth. New places and additions reserve real materials, require pickup/delivery and builder labor, and complete over work shifts. No people means no construction progress. Only completed usable space satisfies current housing needs; planned capacity is recorded separately. |
| LIV-03 | Meaningful residents. Needs determine eating/rest/care; working hours and jobs produce food, wood, stone and tools. Builders/haulers affect sites; care needs a healer present. Positions follow street routes and bridge crossings. |
| LIV-04 | Generations and conditional population. Newcomers need a vacant completed home and food/wellbeing. Households can have children; children mature; people age and die. Capacity, stocks and population remain bounded and nonnegative. No guarantee of endless positive growth. |
| LIV-05 | Inspect every living subject. Search residents, inspect households/needs/current tasks and follow them. Inspect all places, construction/delivery and inhabitants. Departed residents referenced in journals show a history message rather than a broken inspector. |
| LIV-06 | Visible world changes. Actual snapshots drive articulated walking/work/eating/social poses, smaller children, cargo/vehicle activity, different building forms, staged additions/conversions/salvage, roads and environmental effects. No prebuilt distant city or untracked shipment animation masquerades as simulated growth. |
| LIV-07 | Witness records. Journal records births, arrivals, deaths, weather, construction and meaningful changes of use; subjects can be located. Population/development history reflects actual counts, retaining up to 120 daily samples and 100 recent events as declared in LIMITS. |
| LIV-08 | Durable local state. Schema-4 export/import continues the same causal world. Schema-2/3 saves are strictly validated against their retained contracts before explicit migration; existing identities, plots, inventories, cargo and transport experience remain intact. Invalid inputs must not be made acceptable by defaults. Strict fields, finite/bounded numbers, size/array/reference checks reject bad data before replacement. Reset/import require confirmation; cancellation preserves the running world. Schema-1 saves remain untouched/exportable with an archived compatible game. |
| LIV-09 | Time away. Elapsed wall time advances on return, capped at seven real days. Recovery is batched with progress and backdated checkpoints that preserve unfinished elapsed time on reload. Clock rollback contributes no negative ticks. Closed-page execution is not claimed. |
| LIV-10 | Protect saves. Autosave approximately every ten seconds and on page hide; quota/corruption/other-tab changes do not silently overwrite data. Export remains available without storage. No credentials or external data transmission. |
| LIV-11 | Clean game interface. Full viewport, slate/green/copper textured surfaces, temporary panels, 360° orbit/pan/zoom, Street/Above/frame, HUD hide/return and fullscreen. At 375 px, no document overflow; panels scroll internally and following dismisses the panel. |
| LIV-12 | Accessibility and packaging. Named controls and native dialogs, keyboard observation shortcuts, visible focus, readable guide, reduced ambient motion. DOM lists provide access without picking tiny 3D meshes. Single-file artifact embeds its assets and notices and passes syntax/size checks. Full nonvisual equivalence is not assumed. |
| LIV-13 | Reliable long-term work. Reproduce the old default-seed delivery stall and verify construction/development progresses beyond day 90 under viable conditions. Jobs retain pickup/delivery progress through interruptions and use spatial dispatch. Unreachable or starved projects trigger reassignment, alternative work, recovery or a recorded reason; two unfinished projects cannot indefinitely block all development. Test multiple seeds and distinguish genuine resource/demographic decline from a scheduler deadlock. |
| LIV-14 | Conserved cargo. Each load has a source, destination, resource, quantity, mode and stage. Reserving does not duplicate stock; pickup transfers stock into transit; unloading transfers it to its destination. Production, imports, consumption, salvage and losses reconcile the resource ledger. Porters/carts/barges obey their route/capacity rules; dock handling and berth queues cost modeled work/time. Interrupted work and save/reload cannot duplicate or discard cargo. |
| LIV-15 | Persistent building evolution. Floors/extensions and conversions preserve building IDs, parcel/history and valid occupant references. Costs, available parcel/structural limits and labor constrain change. Vacancy/condition can lead to repair, reuse or salvage; recovered material enters inventory with explicit losses and cannot be counted twice. Verify at least one addition and one reuse/salvage lifecycle with save continuation. |
| LIV-16 | Stable, smooth residents. Array insertion/removal/reordering cannot change an existing resident's visual offset, appearance or snapshot matching. Interpolation uses one timestamp/tick timeline and stays continuous across normal snapshots; route direction and follow camera transitions are smoothed. Frame-time performance is measured separately from source-level identity/timing correctness. |
| LIV-17 | Connected sky/climate. A deterministic 192-day year and 48-day seasons drive smooth Sun/Moon positions, changing lunar phase/daylight and seeded weather. Temperature, rain/snow, mud and river state affect relevant production/transport decisions. Ice depends on sustained cold history, not a winter label. Opening/closing a route causes workable job recovery. The environmental model is a game approximation rather than a calibrated forecast or real ice-load safety model. |
| LIV-18 | Explicit learning. A small bounded tabular travel action-value model updates from actual completed deliveries, including elapsed interruption/recovery cost, persists through export/import and influences later choices only within legal physical rules. Incomplete jobs are not rewarded; failure rates require separate evaluation. Its data, outcomes, fallback and evaluation are specified in model.md. No claim of a trained neural model, universal intelligence or proven improvement is made without comparison evidence. |
| LIV-19 | Scalable observation. Target 1,024 living people and 512 places with bounded storage and recent records. Simulation work is isolated from rendering where workers are supported, with an explicit bounded fallback. Only changed world/UI information should require rebuilding. Benchmark frame-time distributions and recovery responsiveness at representative scale; a high average FPS alone does not verify absence of stutter. |

| LIV-20 | Outward neighborhoods. On a viable mature imported city, new construction increases occupied land span and opens distinct connected neighborhoods rather than only adding floors. Save continuity preserves existing plots/IDs; new construction respects unique legal plots. Resource shortages and the 512-place limit remain real constraints. |
| LIV-21 | A city of different uses. Education, civic administration, markets, food processing/storage, water, safety and hospitality have real staffed effects. Renderer silhouettes distinguish these uses and low/midrise housing forms; additions need not become identical towers. Inspect/filter each type. |
| LIV-22 | Visibly changing atmosphere. Normal Orbit shows a distant sky and flat moving celestial bodies behind the world, never floating over city geometry. Moon rendering fades out in bright day; phases still follow the shared calendar. Day/dusk/night colors, stars, cloud motion, wind-driven drops, splashes/ripples, snow/storm effects and seasonal land/foliage changes must be apparent. The Above camera need not show celestial bodies outside its field of view. Shared environment state drives visuals. Browser evidence must include more than a single Sky screenshot. |
| LIV-23 | Observable online learning. Actual legal development decisions and subsequent outcomes train a bounded saved contextual policy, with transparent reasons, pending/evaluated records, sample counts and learned values in City mind. Transport experience remains visible. Tests prove causal updates, action masks and deterministic save continuation; a held-out baseline comparison reports both gains and regressions without claiming universal intelligence or superiority. |
| LIV-24 | Safe visible crossings. Non-boat residents and carts remain on land or bridge decks; safe winter ice is clearly rendered as solid ice. Pedestrian interpolation and display offsets cannot cut across open water. Legacy unsafe routes recover to legal navigation without cargo loss. Actual barge passengers appear aboard boats, not as walking residents on water. |

Budgets: the expanded model enforces at most 1,024 people, 512 places and 160 active shipments, with a 2 MB import bound and a below 1 MB current-game artifact target. These capacities are implementation bounds, not a hardware-performance guarantee; benchmark evidence and any budget adjustment belong in status.md. Recovery work yields rather than monopolizing the UI thread, and renderer capacity must cover every simulated subject. No backend, external AI, analytics or paid service is required. Advanced banking contagion, full ice/fluid/rigid-body physics and coin wagons are outside this accepted core.
