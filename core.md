# Living-world rules and invariants

Canonical implementation: src/colony.js. This deterministic simulation is fictional. A small online tabular transport model learns from simulated deliveries; no external AI, neural model, real city data or calibrated prediction is used. Its exact fields and evaluation limits are in model.md.

The state is schema 4: seed/PRNG state, absolute tick, logical saved time, local inventories, people, buildings/projects, shipments, transport and development learning, a conservation ledger, recent history and lifetime totals. Strict retained validators migrate schemas 2 and 3 before initializing new learning. One tick is ten fictional minutes; two ticks per real second gives 72 seconds per day. There is no final day or historical-frame cache. Snapshots own nested copies; current and one predicted next snapshot support rendering without allowing inspection to mutate the model.

New plots are selected from unused legal land near the connected settlement frontier, balancing named district hubs and industrial/rural uses. Prosperous mature cities also build lower-density neighborhoods and civic services, instead of satisfying all needs through vertical additions. Schools, libraries, markets, councils, mills, granaries, safety stations, waterworks and inns employ residents and provide modeled local benefits. The policy, reward, feedback delay and evaluation limits are specified in model.md.

Founders begin with homes, productive workplaces, shared stores and river piers. Plots expand outward, preserving the earlier plot prefix. Demand leads to new land and additions; a population-dependent project budget replaces the former two-site global gate. Persistent assignments, spatial dispatch and stale-job recovery keep unfinished work from indefinitely blocking the city. Building IDs persist through additions, repair, conversion and salvage, with explicit level, condition, use state, stored materials and project progress.

Resources are food, wood, stone and tools. Reservations retain source stock; pickup transfers a load into cargo, and unloading transfers it to a local store or matching project. A shipment has one reciprocal worker assignment. Death/interruption leaves recoverable cargo. Porter, cart and barge modes have different capacities and route/handling costs; berths queue loading/unloading. resourceBalance compares initial/produced/consumed quantities against all held stock, cargo and building material, with a small floating-point tolerance.

Adults fill nearby productive roles. Low food increases the farming share. Needs and hours influence routines, while assigned work retains progress through interruptions. Paths use streets, bridges, eligible ice shortcuts and river journeys; mud/ice affect legal modes and movement. Jobs produce stock; meals and toolmaking consume it; tools support building. Hunger, exhaustion and social activity affect wellbeing. These are game rules, not a complete human/economic model.

Each day ages residents, selects weather, processes deaths, considers births and newcomers, reassigns available work and records actual population. Households pair adults with spare room; births require food, age/capacity and family conditions. Children cannot work until maturity. Adult children can establish households when space is available. Age is a compressed fictional lifecycle counter, not real human years. Death and departure can lower population. Historical parent IDs may reference deceased residents; current partner references are reciprocal and live.

environmentAt(tick, seed) supplies a fictional 192-day year, 48-day seasons and approximately 29.53-day lunar cycle. Moving Sun/Moon, daylight, temperature, rain/snow, cloud, wind, mud and river level/ice share one deterministic calendar. Production and transport use the environmental state; sky/climate are approximate, not forecasts or ice-safety guidance. Building forms and additions derive from persistent data. Stable resident IDs determine appearance and sub-street offsets; those offsets do not alter route/resource calculations.

Bounds are explicit: 1,024 living people, 512 places, 160 shipments, 100 recent events and 120 daily samples. Capacity/resources constrain expansion; reaching bounds does not stop time. Growth can slow or reverse. Full banking contagion, ownership disputes, rigid-body cargo/fluid dynamics and warfare are outside this implementation.

Persistence validates exact fields, numeric/array bounds, legal plots and building transitions, household capacity, reciprocal family/cargo references, unique shipment ownership and material conservation. Strict schema-2 validation precedes migration, which preserves existing identities/history and accounts for previously paid site materials. Before automatically overwriting old browser data, the app retains its raw pre-migration save locally.

The embedded worker advances within a small time budget and publishes timestamped current/next snapshots on tick changes. Checkpoints include queued and fractional elapsed time, so reload resumes pending life. The page saves recent authoritative checkpoints, with the same loop as a worker-unavailable fallback. Seven real days of catch-up is a local-computation cap; continuous closed-page execution would require hosting.

Historical compatibility: src/simulation.js retains the schema-1 engine; it is not imported by the living-world app. The prior compatible single-file game is archived separately. Earlier comparison outcomes are not recast as colony history.
