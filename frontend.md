# Observation interface and renderer

The full-screen 3D world remains the opening surface. The textured green/slate/copper HUD adds settlement metrics, a small identity, observation rail, cameras/layers and a live clock/event ribbon. There is no comparison viewport, intervention panel or playback console. Observation does not pause time. This expansion's contracts are LIV-13–19 in [requirements.md](requirements.md); implementation and browser evidence belong in status.md.

LIV-20–24 extend the normal observation view. Celestial bodies must be flat distant sky elements behind the world, with a horizon and terrain occlusion; near-camera discs painted over the city are rejected. The Moon fades out in bright daytime views as an artistic visibility choice, while phase and sky position still use the shared calendar. Night stars, moving cloud layers, short wind-driven rain, surface splashes and snow must be visible without giant white rain bars. Seasonal palettes change land/foliage. Housing is predominantly low/midrise with different footprints and roofs; civic, trade, production and infrastructure silhouettes are distinct. Settlement filters by functional building kind. City mind / L shows measured land span, district services, learned development values, pending decisions and evaluated rewards, plus transport experience. No invented outcome is displayed while feedback is pending.

One temporary panel appears at a time. Residents exposes every living person through name/occupation search, with current needs, household, task, destination and cargo where applicable. Settlement exposes places, usable space, building condition/use, active construction/additions/conversions and material needs. Chronicle reports actual development events and daily history with subject links. World contains aggregate stocks, lifetime totals, portable saves, recovery and confirmed reset. A departed or unknown subject shows a history message rather than a broken inspector. Lists and selection use permanent IDs, and ordinary live updates preserve search text, panel position and focus.

The rendered city must show functional variety rather than only color changes: housing, farms, woodland/stone production, workplaces, storage, care/gathering places and river handling. Buildings retain IDs while additions, floors, changed use or salvage alter their components. Stored variation supplies consistent footprints, roofs, materials and facade composition. Site/building details must report actual state; a completed-looking roof or cargo animation cannot stand in for missing modeled work.

Cargo is shown from real model records. Porter/cart/barge modes have appropriate visual forms, and dock queues/loading stages remain observable. Cosmetic crate count or vehicle size is not an independent resource balance. Selected-person routes, carried loads and destinations agree with the corresponding snapshot. Building geometry updates are driven by meaningful structural/condition changes rather than rebuilding the entire city for every stock-count update.

Resident interpolation, appearance and sub-street offsets are keyed by permanent resident IDs. Deleting a neighbor cannot move a survivor. One timestamp/tick interpolation timeline drives positions, walking gait and activity; facing and follow-camera transitions are smoothed along the modeled route. Childhood scale, work props and task gestures are presentation derived from state. Display offsets separate residents at shared street nodes without changing simulated routes or cargo accounting.

The pure environmentAt(tick, seed) module supplies the same smooth environment to the model and renderer: a 192-day year, 48-day seasons, a 29.53059-day lunar cycle, moving Sun/Moon, daylight, temperature, cloud/precipitation, wind, mud and river level/ice state. Directions are y-up world vectors. Moon visibility/illumination follows phase and position rather than a fixed nightly sprite. Visual weather, ground/water appearance and environmental labels must agree with the simulated condition. Decorative foliage/water/smoke are distinguishable from modeled crops, stock and transport consequences. This is a fictional approximation, not an ephemeris, weather forecast or ice-safety tool.

Simulation work runs in a worker where supported, exchanging bounded snapshots with the main thread; an explicit bounded fallback preserves behavior where that path is unavailable. Frame interpolation remains responsive between model updates. Large populations, recovery batches, geometry rebuilds, synchronous persistence and UI refreshes require frame-time profiling rather than an average-FPS claim. The scale target is 1,024 residents/512 places; measured evidence and its limits are in status.md. Canonical enforced limits are owned by the simulation. Overview fog scales with camera distance to keep the expanded settlement readable.

Controls remain orbit/pan/zoom, Street, Above, Sky and settlement framing, resident following, Natural/Journeys/Wellbeing overlays, HUD hide/return and optional fullscreen. Camera exploration does not change simulation choices. Native named buttons/dialogs, visible focus, normal form keys and a readable field guide provide keyboard access. DOM lists expose subjects independently of 3D picking. Mobile panels scroll internally; following dismisses the panel at narrow widths. Reduced-motion disables nonessential ambient motion. Hidden views skip GPU work while elapsed world time remains recoverable.

WebGL/worker/storage/import failures must be readable and preserve the last recoverable world. Strict migration/validation happens before replacement, with confirmation for import/reset and guarded saving for corruption or other-tab conflicts. Browser, touch, screen-reader and GPU verification remain separate evidence; implementation text alone does not verify those experiences. Interiors, first-person collision physics, elaborate banking, full river-fluid/ice simulation and physical execution of every accounting operation remain outside this interface scope.

The game uses an approximate calendar, not a calibrated ephemeris or weather forecast.
